01 · About the project

Turning biomass lignin into a fuel research question

Lignin is a carbon-rich aromatic component of plant biomass. This project will investigate whether lignin-derived pyrolysis liquid can be assessed as a bio-additive in diesel blends, with suitability determined through fuel-property and engine testing.

A

The challenge

Diesel engines depend on petroleum-based fuels. Alternative components require careful assessment of physicochemical properties, compatibility, combustion and emissions.

B

The approach

Connect lignin extraction and controlled pyrolysis with liquid-product characterization, selected diesel-blend preparation and comparative CI-engine evaluation.

C

The potential

Explore a value-added use for biomass-derived lignin while making no assumptions about engine benefits before experimental evidence is available.

03 · Research

The research in brief

Official abstract submitted to the Renewable Energy Research Poster Challenge 2026, with the project's focus areas and proposed validation path.

Extraction and Utilisation of Lignin as Bio-Additive in a Compression Ignition Engine Fuelled with Diesel Fuel – An Approach to Improve Fuel Properties

Srinath Vatchavari Venkateshan, Aarya Brat Shankar & R. Bhuvaneshwaran

Department of Mechanical Engineering · Department of Biotechnology · Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, KARE, Krishnankoil, Tamil Nadu, India

Presenting author: Srinath Vatchavari VenkateshanThematic area: Bioenergy & Waste-to-Energy

Background / Problem

Diesel engines depend heavily on petroleum-based fuels, creating a need to explore alternative fuel additives. Lignin, a carbon-rich component of biomass, offers potential for conversion into liquid products through pyrolysis. However, the suitability of lignin-derived bio-additives for diesel blending requires evaluation of their fuel properties, combustion behaviour and engine emissions.

Objective / Research Question

To extract lignin from suitable biomass, produce bio-additives through controlled pyrolysis, and evaluate their physicochemical properties and suitability for blending with diesel at different dosage levels.

Key Findings / Results

The study will investigate lignin extraction from suitable biomass and the production of bio-additives through controlled pyrolysis. The resulting liquid products will be characterized for density, viscosity, calorific value, flash point and chemical composition. Diesel blends containing different bio-additive proportions, up to a maximum of 20%, will be evaluated for fuel properties, engine performance, combustion characteristics and emissions. Experimental comparisons with conventional diesel will help assess blend suitability and identify a promising dosage level.

Novelty & Potential Application

This study integrates lignin extraction from biomass, controlled pyrolysis, bio-additive characterization and diesel-blend evaluation in a compression-ignition engine. It explores the potential to valorize biomass resources and partially substitute petroleum-based diesel. Further optimization and pilot-scale assessment could help determine its technical feasibility, scalability and contribution to India's renewable energy development.

Keywords: Lignin, Bio-additives, Pyrolysis, Diesel Blending, Compression-Ignition Engine.

04 · Sustainability & impact

Waste-to-value, to be evaluated

Biomass valorisation could support a more circular use of agricultural and plant residues. The project is designed to generate evidence before any impact is claimed.

♻

Waste valorisation

Explore a value-added use for biomass residues instead of disposal, starting from lignin recovery.

⚡

Renewable fuel research

Investigate whether lignin-derived liquids could partially contribute to diesel blending, subject to validation.

◎

Circular bioeconomy

Connect biomass processing, materials and energy research within a circular, resource-conscious approach.

Planned validation milestones

  1. 01Fuel property characterisation of recovered lignin products.
  2. 02Diesel blend preparation up to the planned 20% maximum.
  3. 03CI engine performance, combustion and emission testing.
  4. 04Feasibility and life-cycle assessment (planned).
◆
Evidence first: No environmental or efficiency savings have been measured. Any benefits described here are potential outcomes, subject to experimental validation and life-cycle assessment.
